# What's new in version 2.1.2

## Fixed

- Clipping protection in Settings, Equalizer no longer makes everything quieter. It used to lower the whole sound by the largest boost of the equalizer or bass boost, for example by 6 dB with bass boost High, so bass boost only made the other frequencies quieter. The player's volume is applied after the equalizer, so at a normal volume that headroom is not needed: protection now gives it back and sounds the same as without protection. Only when the volume is so high that the boosted bass could clip does it hold the sound just below that point, so near the top of the volume range, louder makes no further difference. At 100 percent and with volume boost it works as before.
- Crossfade between local files and other media that the player itself plays now blends the two tracks evenly. The fade was applied to the player's volume, which grows much faster than the sound, so in the middle of a crossfade both tracks were far too quiet and the next track stayed almost silent for the first third. Equal power now keeps the same loudness through the whole crossfade, and Linear works as described. Spotify crossfades were not affected.

These changes apply to Windows and macOS. Existing settings and saved data are retained.

Download **ApricotPlayerSetup.exe** or **ApricotPlayer.zip** for Windows, or **ApricotPlayer-macOS-arm64.dmg** for Apple silicon Macs running macOS 27 or later. The Mac ZIP is used by the in-app updater.

The Mac app is not notarized by Apple. If macOS blocks its first launch, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, choose **Open Anyway** for ApricotPlayer, and confirm. Use packages from this project's Releases page.
